Mark the bones for the rigger

Click the figure to place joints: the hips first, then down each limb. Name each one, say what it is for, and the rigger fits the skeleton to your marks instead of guessing.

The artist's take
Motions of this rig (run, idle, attack… all live inside the one rig)
attack, jump, hurt… added to this rig as keyframes; the bones and the drawing stay

Just grab the figure: drag a hand or foot and the limb follows (its elbow or knee folds the natural way as you go); drag another joint to turn its bone; drag the hips to move the body. Dragging between keys makes a key there. Drag on the bar to scrub, drag a diamond to retime it. Feet snap to the ground line when near it; drag the line itself to put the ground where the game has it (or press ground = bottom); knees and elbows stop at their limits. Hold Shift to turn a bone in 15° steps or to move the hips along one axis. Onion skins show the keys before (blue) and after (orange). Keys: space plays, ← → step, [ ] jump keys, K adds a key, D duplicates, H holds the previous pose, M mirrors, F flips the selected joint, Delete removes. Click a joint to select it (it stays highlighted); click empty stage to clear.

Re-skin from a drawing

Download the drawing, edit it in Inkscape (paths only), Upload edited, then Re-skin GO!. Layers named after bones (head, spine, armL, handL, kneeR…) are bound to those bones exactly. Only the look changes: bones, limits, ground and every motion stay.
