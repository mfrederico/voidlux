# Centipede

A roguelike snake shooter. You are a centipede that never stops moving. Every segment you pick up is
part of your build (a bullet type, or a parasite). Crash into things and you lose
segments. Lose them all and crash again: toast.

## Run it

**Three channels.** Build and iterate locally; publish to beta for your own phone; publish to stable for playtesters.

```
python3 tools/devserver.py         # http://localhost:8765/  (no caching: a reload always runs the code on disk)
tools/deploy.sh beta               # https://clicksimple.com/centipede-beta/   committed code, any time
tools/deploy.sh stable             # https://clicksimple.com/centipede/        the playtesters' build: only when you mean to ship
```

`deploy.sh` publishes only committed game files (`--force` to publish uncommitted work, marked `-dirty`), stamps the build id (shown under the title on the title screen, so a tester can say which build they played) and versions every script URL so browsers never mix old and new files. Each target folder is mirrored exactly: anything else in it is removed.

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder:

```
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

then visit http://localhost:8000. No build step, no dependencies.

## Rules

- **Bullets**: every segment is one bullet type and can have exactly one bullet in flight. You can never have more bullets out than segments. A segment shows a solid dot when loaded and a ring while its bullet is away. The reload clock starts when the bullet **lands** (hits, expires or returns), not when it leaves, so a point-blank target does not get shot faster than a distant one.
- **Level ramp.** Every bullet deals more per level past the first: tier-3 and tier-4 (the crafted ones and the rare drops) 25% per level (`CFG.LEVEL_RAMP`), tier-1 and tier-2 12% (`CFG.LEVEL_RAMP_LOW`). Monster hp climbs with the region index, but only linearly through level 1: past the crab each further region adds 60% of a region to the hp formulas (`CFG.HP_CURVE`, `Enemies.hpScale`), so a level-3 monster is tough, not eight times a level-1 one.
- **Bullet speed is a stat.** You crawl at about 205 px/s, so a slow bullet (Venom 260, Moth 210) can barely outrun you. The workbench strip shows every bullet's speed next to its range and rate, Hair Trigger adds 15%, and the **Muzzle Velocity** perk adds 35% (`G.mods.bspeed`).

  | Pebble | tiny, quick, back in a blink |

  The table is `BULLETS` in `js/config.js`. A bullet with `minLevel` never drops before that level. Each row has damage, speed, size, range or life, a minimum cooldown, a potency `tier` (1 to 3) and a drop weight within that tier.
- **Body capacity.** You start with room for 3 segments and that is the cap. Picking up a bullet while full puts it in your bag, and you craft your build on the pause screen. Only the Bigger Body dungeon perk raises the cap, and it is always one of the three perks on offer. `CFG.MAX_SEGMENTS` sets the start.
- **The workbench speaks in icons.** Every bullet tile shows its delivery glyph (shot, piercing shot, laser, burst, boomerang, orbiter, moth, bouncer, mine, pulse, trail) with element badges in the corner; the selected bullet and every result card get a stat strip (damage, elements, pierce, burst, blast, wall damage, range, rate) instead of an English sentence; the perk shop shows a glyph and a strip per perk; the buttons are icons (play, plus, swap, cross, bin). Names stay as text, numbers are universal, so the screen reads without English. All glyphs are drawn in `js/icons.js`.
- **Crafting: fusion.** Two bullets fuse into ONE new bullet that takes a single slot, and fused bullets can be fused again. Fusing costs **dots**: every dot you eat in a dungeon is a coin (power pellets are worth 5), and a fusion costs 20 dots for a tier-3 result or 40 for tier 4. The pause screen is the workbench, in two panels (TAB, the tabs at the top, or a sideways swipe on a phone): **BODY** shows the centipede big with the blueprint, the build readout (estimated dps, bullets in the air, element coverage) and the shooting gallery; **FORGE** shows the bag big, the CRAFTING cards for every fusion you can make right now (a paged grid: tap a card, or walk to it with WASD and press ENTER; each card says where its result will land, `→ slot 3` or `→ bag`, and hovering a card lights that slot up), an info card for the selected piece (what it does, its status chain, and two squares: STASH back to the bag and TRASH; a bag bullet gets an ADD / SWAP pill instead of STASH; the bin is also the drop target, and tapping it destroys the selected piece) and a compact body strip along the top so a fusion has somewhere to fly to. A fusion is choreographed (`js/benchfx.js`): the two ingredients fly to the card and collide with a flash and a chord, the result grows out of it and arcs into its slot (the body strip grows to receive it and the bag steps back), the slot pulses, the dps counts up with a `+N dps` floater and the gallery fires a volley. Any key or tap finishes the animation on the spot; the body itself changes instantly. On a phone the text that matters is drawn half again as big, and a two-finger pinch magnifies the whole workbench to read it, springing back when the fingers lift. The full recipe list is not shown in the game; it is the table below. You can also drag with the mouse or a finger: drop a tile on a glowing partner and, if you have the dots, a card asks FUSE or just MOVE it (ENTER / SPACE, or its buttons); without the dots it simply moves, since the fuse would only be refused. A fuse you cannot afford from a result card raises a NEED N MORE DOTS callout in the middle of the screen (any key or tap closes it). Drop a tile on a partner to fuse, drop a bag bullet on a body slot to swap or on the empty end slot to add, drop a body segment on the bag to stash it, or on the trash bin to destroy it, which transmutes it into 2 dots (`CFG.TRASH_DOTS`). Keyboard: WASD or the arrows walk one cursor across the body rows, the bag rows and the crafting cards (past the last card row the grid pages); the body slot a bag bullet would swap into pulses cyan while a bag bullet is selected. C marks an ingredient, C on a partner fuses, DEL destroys, X stashes, ENTER adds a bag bullet or crafts the selected card, `/` or the ? button lists every key.

Regenerate the table with `node tools/readme-recipes.js`. One family is not in the table because it is a rule, not a list: **Bouncer is a modifier**. Any shot-type bullet (lasers, rocks, spears, elements, fused shots, the Beam) + Bouncer = that bullet's **Ricochet** version: the same bullet, one tier up, that reflects off walls, trees and boulders three times, with fresh range and a cleared hit list after every bounce, so it can hit the same enemy again. Explicit recipes win where they exist (Boomerang + Bouncer is the enemy-hopping Ricochet above); trails, pins, orbiters, moths, mines and pulses do not take it. - **Settings** (`js/settings.js`; O, or the cog under the green play button on the workbench; saved in the browser): **Size on touch screens** (100 / 115 / 130%: the view zoom and the HUD and button scale on phones and tablets), **Fullscreen**, the **Guide** (the same thing as the ? button), **Sound** and **Volume** (50 to 150%; one copy of each sound effect plays at a time, so summoning tree after tree does not stack songs, and bullets may overlap twice so a volley still crackles: `SFX.gate`, `SFX.DUR` / `MAX` in `js/util.js`), and **New segment cards**. W/S choose, A/D or Enter change, Esc or O go back; tap rows on a phone.
- **Save and continue** (`js/save.js`, localStorage key `centipede.run`). The run saves itself at every region change, dungeon exit, perk-shop close and workbench close, and the settings page has a **Save run** row that saves right now and shows when the last save was. The title screen shows the saved run (level, region, score, segments) and Enter continues it (tap on a phone); N (or the NEW RUN button on a phone) starts fresh and forgets it. A continue generates a fresh world and drops you at the start of the saved region, held still until you press a direction, with the body, bag, locked build, perks, mega, power, dots, ghosts, score and kills exactly as saved; the region intro (and a boss climb, if it was a boss region) plays again. Dying clears the save: it is a roguelike. God-mode runs are never saved.
- **New segment cards.** The first time you pick up a bullet type you have never held (ever, in this browser), the game pauses and a card swoops in: the tile at full size, the name and tier, the stat strip, a sentence on how it fires (pierces, bursts, ricochets, hops, homes, orbits, lays a trail...) and its element, and the in-game description. Enter, Space or a tap carries on; the WORKBENCH button on the card (P) opens the workbench instead, so you can fit the new piece straight away (play resumes from there as usual); the checkbox on the card (C) turns the cards off for next time, and the settings page turns them back on. Your starting bullets are never shown.
- **Leaving a dungeon, or closing the workbench,** holds the world still until you press a direction (the screen dims and says so), the same way a run starts, so you can look around before anything moves.
- **De-segmenting.** Any hit in the overworld bursts a segment into **what it was made of**, like Sonic's rings: a base bullet lands as itself, a fusion lands as its base bullets plus every dot the chain cost (a Plasma scatters as a Spark, an Ember and 20 dots). What you **crash into** (a tree, a boulder, a tombstone, a cannon, a coffin, a barn, a booth, the hedge, a rolling stone) throws the pieces away from it; what **attacks you** (a bug, a snake, a spit, a boss, the clown car, a mallet, a tongue, the Overlord's laser) sprays them out evenly all round you. Either way they are loose pieces, always yours to pick up, thrown at least half again past the magnet's reach so you turn round for them, kept inside the region (never over its wall, into the hedge or into a lake), and they fizzle away after 10 s (`CFG.LOOSE.time`), blinking for the last three. A ghost's bite in a dungeon still destroys the segment outright, and a Sacrifice spends it.
- **The rebuild preview.** While anything in the blueprint is missing, a REBUILD panel under the mega bar shows a ghost copy of it: each missing fusion as a faded, dashed tile with the base bullets it still needs underneath (bright ones are in hand, faded ones are to find) and the dots the chain will cost (red if you are short). Every bullet on the ground that the blueprint is waiting for wears a spinning gold **wanted** ring. The workbench blueprint line shows the same as tiles: held entries bright with a tick, missing ones faded with "needs Spark + Ember + 20 dots". Code: `Player.rebuildPlan`, `Render.drawRebuildPreview`.
- **The padlock: lock your build.** The workbench has a padlock button (B, or tap it). **Lock** takes the body as it is, in order, as your build: base bullets and fusions alike, because status effects fire in body order (Frost before Fire is a different build from Fire before Frost). Nothing is locked until you lock something (a tip says so the first time you craft). While locked, leaving the workbench after changing the body re-locks the new arrangement (pieces that were already missing when you opened it are kept in their old place while the body has room for them; a fusion you trash is dropped), so the lock always follows what you last set on the bench. Pickups during play are extras: they work, they get de-segmented and recovered like anything else, but they are not part of the build until you lock again. **A locked build owns its empty slots**: lock a 2-segment body with room for 4 and it stays 2 (shorter, harder to hit). New pickups go to the bag instead of the body while locked, **unless the pickup is exactly what the build is missing** (or a part that makes it): that one goes straight into its slot, in order. Lose a piece while a spare of it sits in the bag and the spare takes its place the moment the piece is lost ("A spare from the pouch took its place"). Anything extra on the body after a rebuild is moved to the bag (unless the bag has no room for that kind). To grow, open the workbench, add from the bag, and leave: that re-locks the longer body. **Unlock** forgets the build and stops the rebuild. After every rebuild and pickup the body is **put back in locked order**: each slot takes its matching segment, pulling one from the bag if there is room, extras follow, minions stay the tail. **Tap the head** on the workbench (or V) and the body row becomes a ghost view of the locked build: every slot numbered in order, held pieces bright, missing ones faded and dashed; tap the head again to go back. (The head faces away from the body on the workbench: it is the front.) Lose a piece (a bite, a slurp, a laser, an abduction) and the game **rebuilds it for you** the moment you are carrying the parts and the dots: after every pickup, every dungeon clear and every boss, it checks each missing entry, plans the whole chain (Rapid Laser II needs a Laser and two Bullets and 60 dots, Burst needs two Bullets and 20), and crafts it, bag bullets first, then spare base bullets on the body; a base bullet or fusion that is itself keeping another blueprint entry alive is never consumed. The result lands on the body when there is room, else in the bag. A banner says REBUILT and the message names what it cost. The workbench shows the locked build as a row of small tiles in body order next to the padlock (held slots bright, missing ones faded with what they still need, or 'find one' for a base bullet); unlock (B) when you are saving dots for something else. Trashing a fusion on the workbench removes it from the blueprint: you did not want it. Code: `Player.blueprint`, `lock` / `unlock`, `snapshot`, `arrange`, `partsOf`, `plan`, `build`, `rebuild`; `Build.open` / `close` / `toggleGhost`.
- **The workbench on a phone.** Tap a tile to select it; tap it again and it does the obvious thing: a bag bullet joins the body (or swaps with the selected body segment when the body is full), a body segment goes to the bag. Drag still works, and the result cards still craft with a tap. **Fusion results go to the body first**: if either ingredient is on the body the result takes that slot, and two bag bullets fuse onto the body whenever there is room (`Player.craft`). The body rows wrap at ten tiles (thirty segments is three rows), clear of the bin. The **AUTO** button (Z on a keyboard) fills the empty body slots from the bag with what suits the build: it never replaces a segment you placed, and it favours bullets that share the body's main element, so a freeze build gets Frost before a stray Ember (`Build.auto`).
- **The shooting gallery.** The strip under the result cards is a live lane: your head fires at a training dummy with the body's real cooldowns, bursts, twins and cascades, and the dummy burns, freezes, poisons and sparks the way a monster would (`js/gallery.js`, a light simulation, not the live Combat system). Its header is the build readout: **measured dps** (damage landed over the last four seconds), bullets in the air at once, element coverage, dummies knocked out. Swap a bullet and watch the lane change. The lane stands for 420 px of range whatever its pixel width.
- **Piping (incidental).** Power also leaks head to tail: a segment inherits a minor status from the segment ahead of it, shown on the workbench as a row of chips under the segment: a ringed **+** chip for the pipe, then the statuses the bullet itself inflicts (burn, freeze, spark, poison, blind, berserk, ricochet). Select a body segment and the STATUS CHAIN shows the segment ahead and what it hands down, the segment with its own statuses, and what it passes to the one behind (`Render.benchChain`). The tags (`PIPES` in `js/config.js`, applied in `Combat.applyPipe`): **+chill** (behind Frost: hits slow the enemy for 1.2 s), **+jolt** (behind Spark: hits stun for a moment and arc), **+singe** (behind Ember: hits leave a small burn, 1.5 s at 20% of the bullet's damage per second), **+taint** (behind Venom: hits leave a small poison, 1.2 s at 25% of the bullet's damage per second), **+dazzle** (behind a laser: hits blind the enemy for 0.8 s so it cannot shoot), **+split** (behind a Splitter, a Cascade or a Shatter: every hit, every hop of a chain and every moth landing throws two shards at a third of the damage, six per bullet at most), **+ricochet** (behind a Bouncer, a juggling pin or any Ricochet bullet: this segment's shots and moths reflect off walls, trees and boulders twice, `CFG.PIPE_BOUNCES`, with fresh range after each bounce, so a Laser, a Spear, a Burst, a Gas Bomb or a Moth behind a Bouncer ricochets around a dungeon; a bullet that already bounces, or one that does not fly, gets the element tag instead). A segment that already carries that element gets no tag. No slot, no cost; reorder with Q/E (or drag) to pipe what you want.
- **Bag.** A bullet picked up with a full body goes into the bag instead of being lost. The bag holds **20 kinds** of bullet (`CFG.BAG_MAX`): a stack of one kind counts once, and each stack holds at most **20** (`CFG.STACK_MAX`). A 21st Pebble or a 21st *kind* is left on the ground, greyed out; a full stack's count turns gold on the workbench (it shows items and kinds, e.g. BAG 44 · 20/20 kinds). Loose pieces of your own body always fit. The bag is shown as stacks in alphabetical order, so a stack never moves when you trash or place one of it. Swap bag bullets in on the pause screen.
- **Armor.** Stinkbugs and the Hornet Queen are armored: plain bullets do 35% and 50% damage. Elements and combos ignore armor, and Plasma does extra. Bring elements. The King Crab has no shell armor; its defence is dodging, stones and parasites.
- **Megas.** Kills charge the power bar under the stats panel (8 + 4 x enemy tier per kill, 120 to fill). When it is full, E unleashes your equipped mega and empties the bar. You collect megas into a **mega deck**: everyone starts with Sacrifice, the perk shop offers one mega you don't own yet (2 ghosts for your second, 2 more for each after), and unique monsters sometimes drop one in the wild (35%). One mega is active at a time: switch it on the LOADOUT page (tap a chip, or WASD + ENTER), which empties the bar. The six: Sacrifice, Hive Mind, Deep Freeze, Bullet Storm, Lightning Storm (curl into a rising, untouchable ball of lightning spikes for 5s) and Will of the Lich (your segments fly off as untouchable flaming skulls for 3s, burning and drying out what they hit, then come home).

  | Mega | What happens |
  | --- | --- |
  | Sacrifice | time stops, the screen flashes, and your tail segment detonates: a huge blast that also breaks walls |
  | Hive Mind | every parasite nearby, riders included, switches sides and spits at your enemies for 8s, then explodes |
  | Deep Freeze | everything on screen freezes solid for 5s and takes extra damage |
  | Bullet Storm | for 4s every segment fires nonstop, ignoring the one-bullet rule and most of its cooldown |

  The table is `MEGAS` in `js/perks.js`, with the effects in the `Mega` object next to it.
- **Burst** (tier 2) fires a three-round burst and all three must return before the segment reloads.
- **Never unarmed.** If no segment can fire, because you're down to just the head or every segment is jammed, the head spits pebbles until something on the body can shoot again. `CFG.HEAD_SPIT` picks the bullet.
- **Loot only drops from kills.** Every enemy has a loot entry: drop chance, tier, and number of rolls. Gnats and beetles drop tier 1 (pebble, bullet), snakes, stinkbugs and hornets drop tier 2 (rock, laser, boomerang, the four elements), the queen drops three tier 3 rolls (orbiter, moth, bouncer, mine, splitter, pulse). Every drop can roll one tier up or down, and four kills without a drop guarantee one. Ramming a fragile bug counts as a kill.

- **Parasites** are tough purple spiders that creep toward you, then ramp up to a sprint over three seconds once they have you in sight. You hear them chitter faster and louder as they close in, and the screen edges pulse. Each one picks a random segment and sprints at it. Once it bites, it feeds (pulsing, with droplets pulled out of the segment), and the bitten segment **and every segment behind it** stop firing (an X on each, a `!` in the HUD chain, a purple throb) until the parasite runs out of time and dies. Jammer feeds 5s, Leech 6s, Slug 7s and slows you 15% meanwhile. A bite near the head is the nightmare case, so shoot them on the way in. They have hp and your segments auto-target them, so kill them on the way in. A Cleanse or a dungeon clear shakes them all off. The deeper you go, the more of them spawn and the faster they run, but their share of the ongoing spawns is capped at 30% (`CFG.PARA_SHARE`; it used to climb to 80% in the jungle) and a fresh region seeds at most six.
- **Minions ride your body.** A tamed parasite joins the tail as an extra **minion segment** that takes **no body slot** (the cap counts weapons only), spits bullets at the nearest enemy from wherever it sits (0.7 x power, about twice a second), and counts as a hit point like any segment: minions sit at the tail, so they are the shield that wears down first. You get them four ways: **head-butt a frozen parasite** (freeze it with Frost bullets, Deep Freeze or an Icewalker trail, then run the head into it), a **Broodmother**'s egg (it hatches straight onto your tail; it waits on the ground if the roster is full), the **Brood** perk (+1 slot and one hatches on the spot), and **Hive Mind** recruits that outlive their eight seconds while there is room. The roster starts at two slots (`CFG.MINIONS.max`). The one time a minion leaves the body: a boss, a named unique or a beefy rare within 320 px makes the tail minion **leap off and dive** at it as a diver (one dive every six seconds), detonating on contact for a big blast; enemies can swat a diver on the way. On the workbench a minion is a tile you can look at, reorder or trash, never bag or swap. They are pseudo-bullets in `BULLETS` (ids `minion_<parasite>`) so every piece of body code reads them as segments. Code: `js/minions.js`.
- **The Siphon** is the device in every dungeon: a squat brass pump sitting on a plain-dot cell deep in the maze (at least half the longest corridor distance from the entrance, `CFG.SIPHON.minDist`). Touch it once and it runs for 7 seconds, vacuuming up to 35% of the plain dots that are left (`CFG.SIPHON.share` / `time`), nearest first, so the maze visibly drains toward it; every dot it swallows is yours and counts toward the clear. The catch: while it runs **every ghost homes in on the pump instead of you**, so start it and leave, and clear the far side while they crowd the noise. It never takes power pellets, and when it is done it turns grey and smokes. It is not finished for good, though: **eat as many ghosts as the maze holds** (two in a Burrow, six in the Nightmare Maze) and the pump recharges. A recharged Siphon does not stay put: it turns into a little mining cart and trundles along the corridors to the floor cell with the most dots within seven steps (`CFG.SIPHON.reach`), so in a big maze its next run lands where the dots actually are. Touch it again once it settles. Minions keep foraging alongside it.
- **Rare and unique monsters.** Any spawned snake or bug can roll an affix (5% in region 1, +1.2% per region, capped at 20%; `CFG.RARE`): **Giant** (half again as big, triple hp, hits harder), **Swift** (60% faster), **Armored** (plain bullets do 30%), **Venomous** (shoots twice as often with extra shots). Rares glow gold, carry their name, always drop loot one tier up, and scatter 2 to 4 dots. Named **uniques** (`UNIQUES` in `js/config.js`, one of each alive at a time, rolled per region): **Old Grey** (region 3+, an ancient armored 14-segment snake; two tier-3 drops and 10 dots), the **Dot Hoarder** (region 2+, a fat beetle that runs from you and never shoots; corner it for 14 dots), and the **Broodmother** (region 3+, a huge parasite that keeps birthing hatchlings and keeps her distance; drops a minion egg and 6 dots).
- **Summoning.** Circling a thing **once** (within about 150 px; a ring fills as you go and the thing starts to rattle) summons its power: a boulder wakes, a tombstone gives up its ghost, a jungle tree sings, a circus cannon fires you, a barn lets its tractors out. The **Summoner** perk halves the lap and makes what you summon 50% stronger (boulder and cannon damage, charm and song time); it stacks (`Perks.summonLaps`, `Perks.summonPower`). Laps are counted as angle swept around the thing; leave for more than 1.5 s and the count resets.
- **Waking boulders.** Summon a boulder and it wakes up: it gets a face, rolls at the nearest monster within 900 px at 280 px/s, flattens trees on the way, bounces off the hedge, and detonates on contact for a 150 px blast of 45 x power damage; it also smashes into region walls. With nothing to hit it crumbles after 8 s. Woken rocks stop being obstacles. Numbers are `CFG.ROCK`.
- **Pickups drift to you.** Any pickup within 10% of the screen width (`CFG.MAGNET_BASE`) is pulled toward the head, faster the closer it is, so you never have to orbit a dot to land on it. Loot Magnet stacks widen the reach (18%, 30%, 46%, 66%...).
- **Dots outside dungeons.** Every ordinary kill has a 12% chance (times Lucky Shell) to drop a single dot; rares and uniques drop piles. Dot pickups look like the dungeon pellets, bigger when they are worth more, and the Loot Magnet pulls them.
- **Density** ramps with the level: each level cleared adds two to the number of enemies kept alive in your region and to the number seeded in new regions, and shortens the spawn interval (`CFG.DENSITY`, `CFG.DENSITY_LEVEL`).
- **Obstacles** have power. Trees cost 1 segment and snap. Boulders cost 2. Lakes slow you. Thorn hedges and region walls cost 1. Trees and boulders also stop bullets, yours and theirs, so shoot around them or use them as cover. A boomerang that hits one turns back early; a bouncer ricochets.
- **Bugs** come in five sizes and all shoot back. A red ring around a bug means a shot is winding up. They burrow up out of the ground, so a dirt mound is a warning.

  Tuning lives in the `BUGS` table in `js/config.js`: hp, speed, power (segments you lose on contact), range, cooldown, shot pattern, drops, and a `weight(region)` function that sets spawn odds.

- **Boss music.** A sinister synthesized loop (bass riff, kick, hat, sparse lead, all oscillators in `Music`, js/music.js, with the tunes in js/music-themes.js) starts when the crab rises. At 50% hp it steps up a semitone, at 30% another, at 20% it goes to fever pitch (a third semitone, faster, brighter, a rising snarl), and under 10% it cuts dead: the crab is terrified, stops throwing and runs from you at 1.7x speed.
- **The crab has a voice**: a stomp on every step, a distorted digital scream when it dodges, rolls a stone or just moves about (constantly once enraged), and a roar when it rises and when it hurls parasites. All synthesized in `js/util.js` (`SFX.growl`).
- **Vignette.** Every mode (and the title) darkens softly toward the screen edges: a cached radial gradient stamped each frame (`Render.vignette`), black normally, blood-red and slowly breathing in nightmare.
- **Nightmare.** Beating the Clown Car no longer leads into an endless circus: ENTER takes the run back to level 1 in **NIGHTMARE**, keeping your whole build (body, bag, perks, dots, ghosts). The same five levels and bosses, harder (`CFG.NIGHTMARE`): monsters have 1.5x health, shoot 25% more often and come 4 to a region more; the saucer deck runs 7 waves with dives twice as often; the world sits under a violet dusk and the music plays three semitones lower; the HUD's top line reads ☠ NIGHTMARE in a flickering blood-orange (in the overworld, the mazes and the deck), and the screen's vignette turns a deep breathing red. Every boss is tougher through its usual DPS sizing, and some have new tricks: the **King Crab's** claw shot and lunge keep tracking you and lead the shot; the **Skull King** has six unmarked graves (bare mounds, nothing to summon) and raises **grave ghosts** from them, two at a time (three enraged), that dive at you and burst on contact; the **Clown Car** never runs out of gas, rams straight through you (every segment it crosses comes off, invulnerable or not), and launches its clowns twice as often, mid-charge too. Beat the nightmare Clown Car for the finale (a confetti storm, NIGHTMARE CLEARED) and the title shows ★ NIGHTMARE CLEARED with your best score. The regions past the ninth, once "Nightmare Meadow" and so on, are now "Deep". Trials count a run as won only when the nightmare is cleared.
- **Levels and bosses**: every sixth wall you break leads up. A short climb cinematic takes you to the **Boss Arena**, where the wall onward is sealed until the boss falls. Level 1's boss is the **King Crab**: it sidesteps your shots, keeps three rolling stones orbiting it that soak bullets (rocks and boulders smash them fast), rolls stones at you, and hurls parasites in volleys. Every few seconds it stops and one claw CHARGES (a red ring shrinking onto it, a rising whine, a ! over the shell); then it LUNGES at you if you are close or SHOOTS the claw out on its jointed arm (about 520px, then reels it back) if you are not. Both lock on where you were when the claw lit up: the crab squares up and stops turning, the shot flies at that spot, and the lunge bites with its front only as far as that spot, so moving through the wind-up dodges either (a very long body may not clear the spot in time). From `BOSSES.crab.aimLevel` (3) on, a harder crab keeps tracking you through the charge and leads both. A claw that closes on you SNIPS the centipede at the halfway point: the front half stays, and every segment behind the cut is flung out whole, away from the crab, to be fetched before it fades (10 s). Hive Mind turns a thrown volley into your own army. Killing it fills your mega, drops three tier-3 bullets and a Cleanse, unlocks the wall, and shows the level-complete screen. Boss hp is set when it wakes from an estimate of your build's damage output (`Player.estimateDps`: cooldowns, flight time, pierce, elements, fusions, the level ramp and Twin Shot; about a 42-second fight for the crab, 48 for the Skull King, 52 for the Serpent, 56 for the Overlord and 62 for the Clown Car for a build that hits 60% of the time, never below a floor and **never above a cap**: 24k for the crab, 45k Skull King, 70k Serpent, 100k Overlord, 140k Clown Car, `hpCap` in `BOSSES`), plus more per level, so a weak build is not walled, a strong build gets a real fight, and a monster build melts the boss instead of always getting the same minute (at 15k dps the Clown Car lasts about ten seconds). Bosses **aim along your arc**: stomps, fireballs, venom, rolled stones, the tractor beam and the car's run all target where you will be if you keep turning the way you are turning (`Player.predict`), so orbiting a boss at a steady range walks you into its attacks. A boss **shrugs off ice**: it freezes for at most 1.4 s, then ignores freezes for seven seconds, so no ice build can lock one down for the whole fight. `CFG.BOSS_EVERY` sets the cadence and `BOSSES` in `js/config.js` the stats.
- **Winning a level.** The boss dies, confetti falls (drawn procedurally, not as particles; the particle, arc and floater ageing in `Combat.updateFx` keeps running on every paused screen, since some drawing code puffs smoke while it draws, and `Combat.MAX_PARTICLES` is a hard ceiling; monsters far outside the view are not drawn at all), and the level-complete screen tallies the winnings in icons: score, kills, dungeons cleared, perks, power, dots, ghosts, minions, longest body, and shows the body you crawl on with. You keep everything. (`CFG.WIN_RESET` can shrink the body and cap back to a number of segments instead, the strongest bullets staying and the rest going into the bag via `Player.shrinkTo`; it is 0, off, because the graveyard is hard enough with what you have.) Enter crawls on; the first wall of the next level is already open.
- **Level 2: the Graveyard.** Every region past the crab is haunted (`World.levelOf`, biome `graveyard`; the arena is the `ossuary`): grey ground under drifting mist, charred trees with bare forked branches and the odd ember, bones, skulls, ribcages, toadstools and will-o'-wisps on the ground, a dead grey thorn hedge, mausoleum doors for dungeon entrances, snakes that are skeletons (**Bone Snakes**), and a new monster, the **Flaming Skull** (`BUGS.skull`): a skull wreathed in fire that dive-bombs you, spits fireballs, and shrugs off burns. Bone Spears drop here.
- **Tombstones.** Each graveyard region has nine (`BIOMES.graveyard.graves`). They are solid like boulders (ramming one costs a segment, bullets stop at them). **Summon one** (circle it once within 140 px, a pale ring fills) and a **ghost rises**: every monster within 720 px, bosses excepted, falls under its spell and **fights for you for 10 seconds**, then **bursts** in a 130 px blast of 40 x power damage (`CFG.GRAVE`). Charmed monsters show a spectral glow and a shrinking arc; they chase the nearest enemy, bite it, and shoot it (their shots spare other allies). A grave works once; if nothing is in range the ghost sinks back and the grave stays fresh. Parasites charmed this way follow the Hive Mind path, so a loyal one can stay as a minion. The autopilot and guide suggest a grave when two or more monsters are near one.
- **Cathedral dungeons.** Level 2 dungeons are cathedrals: stone pillars with pointed arches, candles along the walls, coloured light shafts, a rose window on the floor where the ghosts (pale, spectral) start, checkered flagstones. Same mazes, same rules; the HUD calls them "Cathedral Labyrinth" and so on (`Dungeon.theme` / `Dungeon.name`).
- **Level 3: the Jungle.** Past the Skull King the land is an Aztec city under the canopy (biome `jungle`, arena `ziggurat`): deep green with dappled shade, palms and broadleaf trees hung with vines, carved stone heads where the boulders were (they still wake), ferns, flowers, fallen gold, glyph stones and skull poles, temple doorways for dungeon entrances, snakes that are pythons, and the arena boss sits on a stepped pyramid. Its monsters: the **Earwig** (creeps up, then a sudden pincer lunge, then a breather), the **Flying Cockroach** (an armoured swarmer: plain bullets do half), and the **Iguana**, which never moves: when you are within 300 px it winds up (a dashed line shows where the tongue will go), then its tongue flicks out at 950 px/s and whips back. The tip **slurps the first segment it touches clean off your body** (the iguana heals a little), costs the head a segment the usual way, and bites minions. Stay out of the faint ring around it, or kill it from beyond. Its boss is the Jungle Serpent (below).
- **Singing trees.** The jungle's circle trick (meadow boulders wake, graveyard tombstones raise a ghost): summon any jungle tree (circle it once within 150 px, a violet ring fills) and it **sings**: a pan-flute melody over a drum (`Music.song`; the tune is the tracker song named `treesong`, played once as a layer over the level music through `Music.playOnce`, looping to fill the song's seconds, so edit it in the tracker like any other; delete that row and the old randomly-walked flute comes back), notes float up, and every monster within 700 px (bosses excepted) wanders over and stands swaying by the trunk, **hypnotized for 8 seconds**: no shooting, no biting, no tongue, spiral eyes, and it takes 50% more damage (`CFG.SONG`). While the song plays, anything that wanders into range falls under it too and forgets you. A tree sings once. With nobody in range it hums a few notes and stays fresh. The autopilot and guide go for a tree when two or more monsters are near one.
- **The Shovel.** The second dungeon device, in every maze alongside the Siphon (`CFG.SHOVEL`): a spade lying on a dot cell a little way in. Grab it and you have **3 digs**: push into any inner wall (the border and the exit row stay) and the block is dug out, leaving rubble floor, so you can cut straight to the dots or the Siphon, or make an escape. A quick tap of a direction counts as a push. The count shows above the maze. In god mode every dungeon starts with 9 digs. The autopilot picks the shovel up when it is within eight steps but never digs (that is a human decision; its wall pushes are pathing jitter and are ignored).
- **Pickups show their glyph.** A bullet lying on the ground is a hexagon with the same delivery glyph and element badge it has on the workbench, so you can tell a Laser from a Frost before you pick it up.
- **Every monster has its own death noise** (`SFX.death` in `js/util.js`, called from `Combat.kill`): gnats zip, beetles pop, stinkbugs burp and hiss, hornets buzz down, the queen gets a falling fanfare, flaming skulls whoosh and crackle, earwigs snap-snap-thud, cockroaches crunch and squish, iguanas croak and gulp, snakes hiss-rattle-flop, pythons hiss deeper, bone snakes clatter, parasites squeal and splat, ghosts go "wooo", unicycle clowns honk-honk-crash, jugglers drop their pins, pie clowns splat, elephants trumpet and thud, blobs bloop, kamikaze clowns squeak and pop, chickens ba-GAWK, and the mime dies in silence. All synthesized, no files.
- **Tracker songs.** `editor/tracker/` is an 8-track chip tracker: waves (square, pulse, sawtooth, triangle, sine, noise, arp, wail) and an 8-bit drum kit (kick, snare, hat, open hat, tom, clap, cowbell, zap), patterns of 16 to 64 rows of 16th notes, a play order, tracker keyboard entry, save/load, and **export for music.js**: paste the snippet into `Music.THEMES`, give a boss `music: '<name>'`, or name the song `level2`..`level5` to play it across that level's regions (the boss theme takes over in the arena). `Music.inst` is the synth and is an identical copy of the tracker's, so the two sound the same; `Music.tickTracker` plays the format (`{ tracker: true, bpm, steps, tracks: [{ inst, vol, len, notes }] }`, `_` = rest, `-1` = note off). Mood steps apply to the melodic tracks; drums stay put and get louder at fever pitch. A song named **`title`** plays on the title screen: browsers only allow sound after a key or tap, so the first Enter (or tap) wakes the music and the next one starts the game (the prompt says which). A tracker song saved under a boss's name (`crab`, `skull`, `serpent`, `alien`, `clown`) replaces that boss's hand-written theme, since the later entry in `THEMES` wins.
- **Level music.** A tracker song plays through each level if `Music.THEMES` has one for it, found by number or by name (`Music.levelTheme`): `level1` or `meadow`, `level2` or `graveyard`, `level3` or `jungle`, `level4` or `farm`, `level5` or `circus`. It starts when you enter the level's first region (and on a continue), and a `title` song plays on the title screen. Save a song under one of those names in the tracker and it is in the game.
- **Music** lives in `js/music.js` (the engine) and `js/music-themes.js` (the tunes): `Music.THEMES` has one row per boss (tempo, root note, 16-step bass, optional organ chords, lead, bell, kick, hat); a boss picks its theme with `music: '<name>'` in `BOSSES` (crab, skull, serpent, alien, clown); a tracker song saved under the boss's own id (`crab`, `skull`, `serpent`, `overlord`, `clowncar`) takes over from the classic (`Music.bossTheme`). The crab's is a driving minor riff; the Skull King's is a slow haunting one: a church organ (two detuned voices per note) over a pedal bass, a tolling bell every half bar, and a wailing sine lead with vibrato that shrieks when the fight goes to fever pitch. The mood steps (semitones at 50% and 30%, fever at 20%, silence under 10%) apply to both.
- **The Jungle Serpent** (level 3's boss, `BOSSES.serpent`) is a great feathered snake with fourteen coils and a crest of red, teal and gold feathers. It slithers in a wave around you at range; its coils bite on contact (the head costs two segments, a coil one); it **spits venom** in spreads of globs that fly about as far as you are and burst into **small lingering clouds** (55 px, 2.6 s, drawn with a dashed edge): sit in one and it eats a segment. Every nine seconds **hatchlings** drop off its tail and come for you (three-coil pythons). Under 10% it spits everything it has. Its theme is tribal drums under a pan flute.
- **Level 4: the Farm.** Past the Serpent the land is farmland under a sickly green sky (biome `farm`, arena `landing`): crop rows, **crop circles** flattened into the fields (the arena is one big one), red barns, scarecrows, pumpkins, fences, corn clumps and apple trees for trees, **hay bales** for boulders (circle one and it rolls like a woken rock), crashed probes smoking in the dirt, and a landed saucer with its ramp down for every dungeon entrance. The invaders beam down instead of burrowing up: the **Brainling** (a big wrinkled brain on little legs with a ray gun, keeps its distance), the **Scout Saucer** (a flying disc, armoured, three-shot spread), and the **Abducted Cow** (floats about in a tractor beam, moos, and rams you). Dungeons are **saucer decks**: riveted metal panels, blinking lights, a reactor core at the pen, green orbs for dots. Each invader has its own death noise.
- **More farm.** The **parasites are sheep** here: woolly, black-faced, wearing their parasite colour as a collar, bleating as they sprint at you; same bite, same jam, and a sheep clamps onto the segment it feeds on. **Barns are solid**, and **circling one three times** (like a boulder) flings the doors open: **three tractors** roar out, each at a different nearest monster, and go off like woken boulders (`World.updateBarns`, `openBarn`; they are rollers with `farm: true`). Hay bales still roll like rocks. Every landed saucer wants you aboard: when you are within about 460 px an unused saucer locks a **tractor beam** on you for two seconds and pulls at 150 px/s (you crawl at 205, so you can steer out of it, or let it reel you into the dungeon); a green cone with rings sliding up it shows the pull, and it recharges in seven to eleven seconds.
- **The Brain Overlord** (level 4's boss, `BOSSES.overlord`) stands on a hovering disc: a huge wrinkled dome, black almond eyes, a silver suit, a ray gun, unimpressed. Its moves: a **laser**: a dashed line warns for 0.9 s, then a thick pink beam fires for 1.4 s and sweeps after you, cutting off any segment it crosses (step off the line sideways); the **tractor beam**: a green dashed circle marks where your tail is heading, 0.9 s later whatever is inside is **ABDUCTED** (about every 8.5 s) (up to two segments, never a minion): time crawls to 18% for 1.3 s with green scanlines and a banner, and the segments become the alien's gun, orbiting it and **firing their own bullet type back at you** (enemy shots in that bullet's colour at its full speed, led along your arc from where the segment orbits, a burst bullet firing its burst, about one volley a second per captive; one hit each, no elements) until it dies, when they fall back to the ground as pickups; the **saucer landing**: the same shadow telegraph as the Skull King's foot, then its saucer sets down on the spot (two segments, no shards); and it **beams down brainlings** beside it. Its theme is a theremin over a creeping square bass. The autopilot steps off the laser line, drags its tail out of the tractor circle and out from under the saucer. Its tractor beam also sucks up whatever lies in the circle (dots, power-ups, ghosts, your own loose pieces): they rise into the saucer, and when the Overlord dies everything it took rains back down.
- **Level 5: the Circus.** Past the Overlord the land is the midway of an evil circus and pure psychedelia (biome `midway`, arena `bigtop`): the ground cycles through every colour, candy stripes breathe across it, spotlights sweep, confetti drifts forever, tie-dye spirals turn and checkerboards pulse. Giant spotted mushrooms with sleepy faces and turning lollipops for trees, beach balls for boulders (they still wake, and roll with a face), **ball pits** for lakes, a turning **Ferris wheel** and a **carousel** with horses going up and down in every region (both solid), candy-floss and toffee-apple stalls, barber-striped poles with pennants, huge clown faces painted on the ground, balloon bunches, popcorn, little tents, ticket booths, bunting for the hedge, and a striped big-top tent for every dungeon entrance. The snakes are **balloon animals** (glossy, knotted every third segment, a knot for a tail). Touch any clown and you press its nose: honk-HONK. Its monsters: the **Unicycle Clown** (zigzags at you, honking), the **Juggler** (lobs pins that ricochet off boulders and walls), the **Mime** (stands still and holds an **invisible wall** across the line between you: your bullets and your body stop at it until the mime is dead; a faint shimmer gives it away), the **Pie Clown** (its cream pies splat on your *screen* for a couple of seconds, harmless), the **Pink Elephant** (a rare, armoured, balancing on a circus ball and bouncing off everything), and **Lava Blobs** (lava-lamp blobs that split in two when they die, until the halves are too small). Kamikaze Clowns pop out of jack-in-the-boxes you crawl past. Juggling Pins drop here.
- **The cannon.** The circus's circle trick: each midway region has two circus cannons (solid, like boulders). **Summon one** (circle it once) and the barrel swings toward the thickest crowd (east if nobody is about), then it **fires YOU**: 1.1 s of flight at 950 px/s, invulnerable, dealing 60 x power to everything you pass through and stunning it; a wall you hit takes 120 damage; you land in confetti (`CFG.CANNON`). The cannon reloads for six seconds.
- **Juggling Pins** (`BULLETS.pin`, level 5 loot) are ricochet bullets: a pin bounces off trees, boulders and walls three times before it drops. Tip one on the workbench: Pin + Ember = **Flaming Hoop**, + Frost = **Frozen Custard**, + Venom = **Pie Pin**, + Spark = **Static Pin** (five bounces, the element on every hit), and Pin + Pin = **Cascade**: five pins at once, every one ricocheting, which is the point of the whole level.
- **Twin Shot** (perk): every bullet you fire brings a free copy beside it at 60% damage. The copies belong to no segment, so they cost no reload. Stacks: a second take adds a third bullet. `G.mods.twin`, applied in `Combat.fire`.
- **The Big Top** (level 5 dungeons): striped canvas blocks in wild colours with fairground bulbs, sweeping stage lights, a ring at the ghosts' pen, popcorn for dots and cotton candy for power pellets (still dots, still coins), and the ghosts are balloon clowns with red noses. Its device is the **Hammer** (`CFG.HAMMER`): grab it and it's **HAMMER TIME**, old Donkey Kong style: for 10 seconds it swings by itself about three times a second (no button), slamming your cell, the cells above and below and the one ahead: any ghost there is **eaten, scared or not** (ghost currency like any eaten ghost), moles are flattened, and the high striker rings as you pass. Its own tune plays while it lasts (`Music.THEMES.hammer`: a frantic fairground loop at 168 bpm) and the HUD counts it down; then the maze's music comes back. **Moles** pop out of dot cells every seven seconds and steal the dot after 3.5 s unless you hammer them (3 dots each; walking onto one just sends it down). The **high striker** is one cell in the maze: hammer it and the bell rings: +1 ghost coin, +6 dots, every ghost scared.
- **Laughing gas.** The Clown Car's runs leave pink clouds behind it. Monsters inside giggle and cannot attack. You inside get **the giggles: the controls are mirrored** for a second (`CFG.LAUGH`), no damage. Don't tailgate the car.
- **The Clown Car** (level 5's boss, `BOSSES.clowncar`, the end of the demo) is drawn side-on so it reads as a car: a bubble-roofed tiny car on two huge balloon tyres, pink with yellow polka dots, a grinning chrome bumper, two big headlights, a flower on the bonnet, a bulb horn, an exhaust pipe, faces crammed in every window, two clowns out of the sunroof waving and one hanging out of the door. It flips to face the way it drives; the bonnet is up and steaming while it refuels. It drives rings round you honking, then **revs** (headlights flash, a dashed line shows its path) and **BURST-RUNS** straight at you at 540 px/s: the head loses two segments, a segment it clips is gone. Then it sputters **OUT OF GAS** and sits refuelling with a pump jack for three seconds and more, taking 60% extra damage: that is the window. The run shortens and the refuel lengthens as it loses hp. Every six seconds its doors fly open and **kamikaze clowns** run out with mallets (one bop costs a segment, then they burst into confetti; they run out of puff after twelve seconds). At half hp the doors fall off and the clowns come twice as often. Its **horn** is a shockwave that shoves you back and **turns your bullets around**; its **flower** sprays a fan of water that pushes you about, nothing more. Theme: a calliope (oom-pah bass, piping square chords, a lead that is slightly wrong) on a tape that wobbles. It dies to a sad trombone and a storm of confetti, and drops two Juggling Pins. The level-complete screen after it says THAT'S THE DEMO; Enter crawls on into an endless, harder circus.
- **Things to discover** (one or two per level, all in `World.updateDeco`): level 1 **beehives** hang from some meadow trees: a bullet that hits the tree has a 50% chance to shake out four angry bees that fight for you for eight seconds, then burst (the grave charm on fresh gnats, `CFG.HIVE`). Level 2 **coffins** (solid) creak open when you crawl up: half the time treasure (a tier-3 bullet and dots), half the time two flaming skulls. Level 3 **geysers** erupt every six seconds for a second: any monster over one is thrown and stunned (22 x power), and you are tossed, unhurt; lure things onto them (`CFG.GEYSER`). Level 4: climb onto the parked **tractor** and plow for eight seconds (the giant mechanic below), and **circle a crop circle** (one lap, like a grave or a tree; the green beam gathers as you go round): a tier-2 bullet beams down into it (once per circle). Level 5: **jack-in-the-boxes** pop a Kamikaze Clown when you pass, the **hall of mirrors** shows your reflection crawling on the far side, and the **fortune teller**'s tent flips a card when you touch it: CHICKENS (every monster within 600 px becomes a harmless, panicking chicken that drops loot when caught), RUBBER CHICKENS (your bullets squeak and hit for half again, twelve seconds), RAIN (dots and bullets fall out of the tent), or THE STAR (your mega charges).
- **Oddity pickups** (circus regions, 70% chance of one per region): **EAT ME** cake makes you enormous for eight seconds: nothing hurts you, you move at 85%, and whatever touches your head is crushed for 45 x power (trees too); the tractor uses the same mechanic. **DRINK ME** potion makes you tiny: enemy shots and tongues sail over you, you crawl half again as fast, your bullets hit for 60%; bodies and bites still count. **The mushroom**: double damage for eight seconds, the whole screen inverts, spiral eyes, a rainbow echo behind you. `CFG.GIANT` / `TINY` / `SHROOM`.
- **The Skull King** (level 2's boss, `BOSSES.skull`) is a giant skull with a crown of bone spikes, floating on a cushion of fire. It hovers at range, breathes spreads of fireballs, calls Flaming Skulls up from the bone pile behind it, it breathes a **ring of fire** every nine seconds (sixteen fireballs rolling outward in a full circle that an orbiting centipede has to find a gap in), and its signature move is the **STOMP**: a dark shadow with a red dashed ring grows where you are *about to be* (it leads your heading by about a second), the word STOMP flashes, and 1.3 s later a **skeletal foot** slams down from above over a 175 px radius. Anything under it (head or any segment) loses 2 segments; the foot also squashes its own skulls, your minions and charmed monsters, and eight bone shards fly out of the crater. Get out of the shadow. The boss music steps up as it weakens, and under 10% it cuts out and the stomps come in a frenzy. Killing it drops three tier-3 bullets, two Bone Spears and a Cleanse. The autopilot steers out of the shadow.
- **Break out**: shoot a section of the thorn wall on the right to reach the next region. Rocks do triple damage to walls.
- **Dread**: the further right you go, the darker the land gets, the trees turn sickly, enemies' eyes go red, and the regions are named Dark, Cursed, then Nightmare. Enemies also get more hp and speed per region. `CFG.DREAD_REGIONS` sets how fast it ramps.
- **The saucer deck** (level 4, `js/deck.js`, on while `CFG.DECK_MODE` is true): the farm's dungeon entrances lead to a fixed-screen shooter instead of a maze. The centipede folds into a cannon at the bottom of the deck: the head on top and the segments racked below it like bowling pins (rows of 2, 3, 4...; a bigger body stands taller). It slides left and right only, and there is no auto-aim: every segment fires straight up the deck (a chain's first hop and a pulse's trigger count only what is right above it, and a moth bends only toward what is already nearly ahead, at half its turn), so you aim by sliding. On the deck every segment fires a **pellet**: a plain round shot straight up in that segment's colour, with no area, pierce, bounce, homing, chaining or orbit, carrying the segment's power (its base damage per second, delivered on a fixed rhythm of its cooldown plus 0.55 s; perks such as Hair Trigger and Brawn still count). A stronger build still clears faster; no build can blanket the deck. Your segments hit at ten times their damage (`CFG.DECK_DMG`) because the invaders keep their overworld health and there are many of them. Five waves of invaders (brainlings, scout saucers, abducted cows from wave two; each wave wider, deeper and faster: 21, 24, 36, 40 and 55 invaders, up to eleven columns by five rows) step down the deck in formation, dropping and turning at the edges and speeding up as they thin; the front of each column fires rays that fall nearly straight down, only a few in the air at once, and a ray knocks off the exact segment it hits (the front rows shield the ones behind). Galaga-style, an invader now and then (a pair, from wave two) peels out with a half loop, swoops down at you weaving and firing, and comes back to its slot from the top; a diver that crashes into the rack knocks off the segment it hits, and a diving cow can lift one. Everything you kill drops dots that drift down to be caught by your head or body; dots, powerups and the UFO's ghost fall off the bottom if you miss them, so you chase them side to side. Your own lost pieces are the exception: they land on the floor and wait 10 seconds (blinking for the last 3). A bonus UFO crosses now and then with its tractor beam on: anything on the deck under it (dots, powerups, your own lost pieces) rises into it. Whatever the beam starts lifting gets a synthesized MOO (`SFX.moo`, at most one every 0.7 s; the overworld's tractor beams and cows moo too). Shoot it down and it bursts in a shower of confetti, flinging everything it took out all round, plus a ghost (a perk coin); let it cross and it keeps them. When the last wave dies, everything still on the deck (and anything a UFO overhead was carrying) streaks to you before the perk shop opens. A cow that reaches your band lifts your tail segment and climbs away: shoot it before it leaves the top and the segment falls back for you. Any invader that reaches the band crashes into you if it touches the cannon, knocking off the segment it hits; one that reaches the floor anywhere else LANDS: it tears off your tail segment (at most one a second, so a whole row touching down together costs one) and is gone, so a wave can never sink out of reach. With no segments left, a landing is the end. A segment you lose on the deck pops its pieces up to be caught. The deck has its own sci-fi tune (`Music.THEMES.deck`: 116 bpm, a synth sequencer through D minor, B flat, E flat and A under a theremin wail, half-time drums and laser zaps); the farm outside plays its own cute 8-bit techno (`Music.THEMES.farm`), which comes back when you leave the deck. The last wave down clears the deck: the perk shop opens as for any dungeon. The autopilot plays it (`Bot.deck`): shoot the cow, dodge rays about to cross the band, take the UFO when it is overhead, catch what falls, else sit under the lowest invader and lead its drift.
- **In a maze** the body enters coiled up on the start cell and unfurls along the corridors you take (it never lies across a wall), and the top-left stats panel fades almost away while you are in the corner under it.
- **Dungeons** scale with the region: early ones are small, open Burrows with two slow ghosts and long power pellets, then Warrens, Catacombs, Labyrinths, and from region 11 the Nightmare Maze with six fast ghosts and almost no loops. The tiers are `CFG.DUNGEON_TIERS` in `js/config.js`. Walk into a cave. The exit is shut for the first three seconds (a countdown shows on it), so a push down on arrival does not throw you straight back out. Steering is on the grid, but it corners like Pac-Man: a sideways press within about a third of a cell of a corner snaps to the corner and turns at once instead of waiting for the exact centre, a press that cannot be taken yet is kept while you hold it (it fires at the next opening), and a released press is forgotten after 0.3 s, so a tap in a corridor never turns you at a junction seconds later (`CFG.DUNGEON.CORNER` / `WANT_GRACE`). A wall **stops** you (it does not hurt, and it does not turn you): the maze waits while you look, little arrows show the open ways, and the next direction you press that is open starts you again. A power pellet scares the ghosts blue; in the last two seconds of the scare they blink blue and yellow five times, so you know when to stop chasing. Eat every dot to cleanse parasites and open the **perk shop**: the game pauses and shows four perks (Bigger Body is always one of them). The clear gives you one free pick. After that, **every ghost you have eaten is a coin** that buys one more perk. Every perk has a **level**: how many times you have taken it in the run, free picks included, and its price is **level + 1 ghosts, for the whole run**: 1 ghost the first time, then 2, 3, 4, 5. The card shows the level and the next price. So stacking one perk costs steadily more (a fifth Potent Elements is 5 ghosts) while a perk you have never taken is always 1 ghost; prices never reset between dungeons. Megas are one-off swaps and stay at 1 ghost. Bought slots restock with a new perk. A/D selects, Enter or 1 to 4 buys, Space leaves. Unspent ghosts carry over to the next dungeon and show in the HUD. Perks stack for the whole run.

  | Perk | Effect |
  | --- | --- |
  | Swift Legs | +15% movement speed |
  | Hair Trigger | -25% bullet cooldown, bullets 15% faster |
  | Muzzle Velocity | +35% bullet speed |
  | Brawn | +50% bullet damage |
  | Long Reach | +30% bullet range and lifetime |
  | Thick Skin | invulnerable 60% longer after a hit |
  | Loot Magnet | widens the base 10% pull to 18% of the screen; stacks accelerate the reach (30%, 46%, 66%...) |
  | Bitter Blood | parasites die twice as fast on you |

  Prices: a perk costs 1 ghost plus 1 per copy you already own, for the whole run; Bigger Body adds n²/6 on top. The dungeon's free pick pays up to 4 ghosts of a price, and you pay the rest (the card shows FREE+3).
- **Balance knobs** live in `CFG.BALANCE` (config.js): the free pick's cap, Bigger Body's price curve, the fusion floor (at load, any fused bullet whose base DPS is under 1.3× its two ingredients' together has its damage raised, up to ×2.5; the walkers are skipped), a shorter after-hit invulnerability past 20 segments, monsters that shoot 4% more often for every 3 segments past the starting 3 (up to +60%; bosses keep their own rhythm), and a crowd that grows with your damage: past 50 DPS, every doubling keeps 2 more monsters in the region (at most 12), they spawn that much faster, and each kill's loot and dot chances shrink in step so loot per minute stays about level.
  | Bigger Body | +1 max segment: an empty slot (always on the shelf; its price climbs faster than the others) |
  | Lucky Shell | +30% loot drop chance |
  | Potent Elements | burn, poison, spark and freeze 50% stronger |
  | Brood | +1 minion slot, and a parasite minion hatches right now |

  The cards arrive like a slot machine: each one spins through random perks and settles on its real one, left to right, with a tick per step, a pop per card and a chord when the last lands. Any key or tap lands them all at once; nothing can be bought until they have landed (`Perks.mkReel`, `Render.drawPerks`).

  The table is `PERKS` in `js/perks.js`; each row is a name, a description and an `apply()` that bumps a multiplier in `G.mods`. Power pellets make ghosts edible.

## Guide and autopilot

**Guide** (press T once, or the ? button on a phone, or `?guide=1`) is for new players. The first time a fusion becomes affordable with the guide on, the game pauses on a TIME TO CRAFT card: the fusion you can make right now, what fusing is and why it matters, and a button into the workbench. It shows once per browser (`Settings.data.craftTip`). The guide it runs the same sensing and goal logic as the autopilot but never steers and never presses a key. It draws a dashed line and a pulsing ring on the thing to go for, with its name ("Pebble (tier 1)", "shoot the wall", "dungeon"), an arrow at the screen edge when it is off screen, a yellow bar saying what to do and why, a craft suggestion when a fusion is affordable, and in the perk shop it pre-selects the perk it would take. It uses scripted rules only, so it works anywhere, phones included.

### Autopilot

Press **T** (or open the game with `?bot=1`) and the centipede plays itself. It is built the same way as the brains in the archived terminal RPG (`archive/game/src/game/brains`): two layers.

- **Reflex** (every frame, scripted, `Bot.world` / `Bot.dungeon`): grabs any pickup within reach and goes out of its way for a bullet while body slots are empty (segments are hit points), takes an unused dungeon on the way east while the body can still grow from it, steers toward the current goal with a potential field (attraction to the target, repulsion from enemies, a wide berth around parasite spiders and the boss, tangent steering around any tree, boulder or stone the chosen direction would clip, a hop off the line of incoming shots, soft walls at the hedge), kites at bullet range when fighting (closer when minions are along, so they engage too; minions, Hive Mind recruits and their shots are never treated as threats), weaves at the east wall so its bullets land, detours when stuck, fires the mega on a boss or a crowd, fills the body from the bag and swaps stronger bullets in. In a dungeon it runs a BFS around the hunting ghosts to the nearest dot, grabs a power pellet when a ghost is close, chases scared ghosts with weapons off, and goes for the Siphon first while plenty of dots remain.
- **Play the saucer deck directly**: open the game with `?deck=1` and start a run. You warp to the farm with a level-4 body and board the Space Invaders deck at once. After it, the farm's other dungeon entrances lead back onto the deck.
- **Balance trials**: open the game with `?trials=100` (optionally `&fast=20` updates per drawn frame, `&model=tev1:0.8b` or `nimble`, `&host=http://localhost:11434`). The autopilot plays that many runs, restarting on death, with sound off and the cards off, and when the last run ends it prints a summary on the title screen and in the console (`console.table` of every run; the same JSON in localStorage under `centipede.trials`): seconds and region per run, what killed it, dungeons and perks, body size, and the saucer deck's clears, time and segments lost. Without a reachable Ollama the strategist falls back to the scripted goals, which is the quicker batch to run first. A run's seconds count only time in play (not the shop or the workbench), and a covered or background window keeps the batch at full speed. Beating the Clown Car (the end of the demo) counts as a win and starts the next run; `&cap=5400` (the default) calls any run that plays longer than that many seconds.
  - **Model vs rules**: each run also logs which perks and fusions were taken and who chose them (a `*` marks a scripted pick, made when the model was offline), and the summary lists the most-taken perks and fusions and the model's share of the picks. `&brain=scripted` never asks the model, for a baseline batch. The model weighs for damage: every fusion and perk it is offered comes with its DPS change, measured by trying it on the live build (`Player.estimateDps`) and undoing it, and each run logs its end and peak DPS. Each batch is also stored under `centipede.trials.<model>`, so a pair survives side by side:
    - `?trials=50&fast=20&brain=scripted`
    - `?trials=50&fast=20&model=tev1:0.8b`
    - `?trials=50&fast=20&model=nimble`
- **Strategist** (event-driven, async): on a new region, a lost segment, a boss, loot nearby, or every 7 seconds, it sends ONE typed request to Ollama's decision endpoint `POST /v1/systemone` with the situation as text (body, bag, dots, ghosts, mega, nearby enemies with hp and distance, pickups, the wall, the entrance) and four questions: a **goal** choice between the goals that make sense right now (advance, loot, hunt, dungeon, flee, boss, each described concretely), a **danger** score (Safe to Critical), a **craft** choice when a fusion is affordable (keep, or one of the results), and a **mega** yes/no when the bar is full. In the perk shop it asks a **perk** choice (each affordable perk with its price, or save the ghosts). The model is `tev1:0.8b` by default (`?model=nimble` for the bigger one, `?host=` for another Ollama). Answers take about half a second to a second here, and the reflex keeps playing meanwhile.

The HUD bar shows the brain, the latency, the goal, the danger rating and the last reason. When Ollama is unreachable the bot says so and plays on scripted rules (retrying every 15 s); note that Ollama only accepts browser requests from `localhost` or `file://` origins unless `OLLAMA_ORIGINS` is widened, so the autopilot thinks on a local copy, not on the hosted one. Your own keys always win: the bot only steers while you are not pressing anything, so you can nudge it. With the bot on, a dead centipede restarts after four seconds and each run is logged to the browser console (`[autopilot] run N: died in region ... killed by ...`) and kept in `Bot.runs`, which makes it a cheap balance tester: leave it running in god mode off and read the causes of death.

## Desktop and Steam

The full step-by-step for Steam (Steamworks setup, depots, uploading, store assets, release, Mac, mobile stores) is in [`desktop/STEAM-HOWTO.md`](desktop/STEAM-HOWTO.md).

`desktop/` wraps the same `index.html` in Electron (the web build is untouched). In development it reads the game straight from the repo; packaged, the game files ride along in `resources/game`.

```
cd desktop
npm install                # once: Electron and electron-builder
npm start -- --no-sandbox  # run it in a window (F11 fullscreen; Ctrl+Shift+I devtools, dev only)
npm run dist:linux         # dist/linux-unpacked (run centipede.sh)
npm run dist:win           # dist/win-unpacked/Centipede.exe (builds on Linux; untested without Windows)
npm run dist:mac           # needs a Mac, and an Apple Developer ID to sign and notarise
CENTIPEDE_SMOKE=/tmp/shot.png ./dist/linux-unpacked/centipede.sh   # headless self-test: plays 5 s, checks saves, writes a screenshot, prints a report
CENTIPEDE_EVAL=scene.js CENTIPEDE_SMOKE=/tmp/shot.png npm start -- --no-sandbox   # stage any scene (JS run in the page) and screenshot it, off-screen
```

In the desktop build the game shows a QUIT square on the title (ESC on the title quits too), FULL SCREEN toggles the window, and alt-tabbing away pauses a run (on the workbench). **Linux** launches through `centipede.sh`, which starts Electron with `--no-sandbox`: Steam's runtime container cannot run Chromium's setuid sandbox helper, and the game only loads its own local files. Use `centipede.sh` as the Steam launch target on Linux and `Centipede.exe` on Windows.

**Uploading to Steam** (SteamPipe): `desktop/steam/upload.sh <login> <app-id> <linux-depot> <windows-depot> ["description"]` builds both platforms and runs `steamcmd +run_app_build`. It uploads without setting the build live; set it live on a branch from the Steamworks Builds page. Not done yet: Steamworks itself (overlay, achievements, cloud saves via `steamworks.js`), controller support for Steam Deck, and the Mac build.

## Phone

On a touch screen everything is about 30% bigger (`CFG.MOBILE_SCALE`): the overworld camera zooms in 1.3x (the screen shows less of the map, drawn larger), dungeons zoom as far as the maze fits, and the HUD panel, messages, banners, boss bar, segment chain, touch buttons and joystick all scale by the same factor (`Render.zoom`, `Render.ui`). In a dungeon the stats panel shrinks to two lines so the maze is not hidden under it. The workbench, the shop and the title screen keep their fit-to-screen layouts.
Touch controls switch on automatically on phones and tablets (coarse pointer), or on the first touch anywhere else. Drag your left thumb to steer, FIRE toggles weapons, MEGA fires the mega, CRAFT pauses and opens the workbench (it turns gold with a count badge whenever a fusion is affordable). Tap to start, tap a shop card to buy. On the workbench, drag tiles: between body and bag, onto another tile to fuse (partners glow), onto the empty slot at the end of the body to add, onto the trash bin (top right, so it takes a deliberate reach) to destroy it for 2 dots. The CRAFT button hides while the workbench is open: the green play button on the right resumes, with the settings cog under it, both under the thumb (there is no mark button: tap a result card or drag tiles to fuse). Tap a result card to craft it. Mouse drag works the same on a laptop.

**Maze controls on a phone.** In the dungeons the left stick's centre trails your thumb (at about half its radius), so a turn is a short roll from wherever your thumb is, not a trip back across the stick; it reads four directions biased toward turning (a lean of 30° off your heading is a turn, a reverse needs a clear pull back), and a turn asked early waits up to a second for its junction. A quick swipe anywhere on the right side of the screen also queues a turn. Keyboard play is unchanged. Tuning: `CFG.DUNGEON` TOUCH_GRACE, TURN_BIAS, LEASH, SWIPE. The build before this change is tagged `pre-maze-touch`.

**Phone layout.** Messages stack in the left column under the stats and the mega bar, newest three, each on a dark pill (a long one wraps to two lines), clear of the play's centre and both thumbs; the keyboard hint line is hidden. The corner buttons (?, ⛶, CRAFT) sit on dark backings with labels sized to the button. The title, the perk shop (taller cards with the whole description), the discovery card and the game-over screen are drawn about a third bigger, and every prompt says TAP rather than a key. The workbench's FORGE tab uses two wide card columns with larger stats and a bigger body row. On the saucer deck a finger anywhere drags the cannon (it slides toward your finger). The game holds a screen wake lock while it is open, so the phone does not dim mid-fight.

**Install.** `manifest.webmanifest` (fullscreen, landscape, icons in `icons/`) lets Chrome install the game to the home screen; launched from there it opens with no browser bars.

**Fullscreen.** The title screen shows a big green FULL SCREEN button on touch devices; the ⛶ button at the top right does the same during play, and the first touch on an Android phone asks for fullscreen by itself. iPhone Safari has no fullscreen API, so the title explains the only route: Share, then Add to Home Screen, and open the game from that icon. **The logical width follows the screen.** The canvas is always 720 tall, and its width is recomputed from the screen's aspect ratio (960 to 1700, `CFG.W_MIN` / `CFG.W_MAX`) at load, resize, rotation and fullscreen. A wide phone in fullscreen therefore sees more of the map, a squarer screen less, and nothing is letterboxed. Every layout reads `CFG.W` at draw time: the HUD, the touch buttons (anchored to the right edge), the workbench, the shop, and the dungeon re-centres itself.
